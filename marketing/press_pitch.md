## Short pitch (email or tip-form body)

Hi [Desk / 7 On Your Side],

I'm writing with a resource, not a complaint, as a follow-up to ABC7's September 21, 2026, 7 On Your Side story on Southern California garage door repair overcharging (reported by Leanne Suter). In that story, a Woodland Hills homeowner said a $97 tune-up text turned into a bill of about $4,000, and an 89-year-old San Clemente owner said he was billed about $12,000 after calling an 800 number. Both cases involved work approved without a written estimate, and one listed address was fake, with no license number to pursue.

I built a free website, CaliDoorCheck (https://calidoorcheck.com), so homeowners can check a garage door company before they call. It lists currently renewed California CSLB D-28 licenses (Doors, Gates and Activating Devices) in Los Angeles, Orange, Ventura, Riverside, San Bernardino, and San Diego counties. The listings come from CSLB's official public List by Classification and County. As of a November 22, 2026 download, the directory has 1,608 licenses in clear status. Each card shows the license number, bond, and workers' compensation information as CSLB published it. The site also has a 60-second license-check walkthrough, a short scam playbook that cites your story and CSLB guidance, and published national price ranges for spring replacement, with sources cited.

CaliDoorCheck is not affiliated with CSLB or ABC7, it does not rank companies by payment, and it does not claim to replace CSLB's Instant License Check — we tell people to re-verify live status there. I run it as Yaro Celis / YCO Productions.

If useful, I can walk a producer through the site, share how the CSLB data is pulled, or help check a company name from a viewer tip against the public license list. I am not asking you to endorse the site. I thought your team and viewers might want to know it exists.
