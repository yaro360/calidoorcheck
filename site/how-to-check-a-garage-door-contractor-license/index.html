How to check a garage door company's license in 60 seconds

Updated 2026-11-19

In California, garage door work is licensed by the Contractors State License Board (CSLB). The classification to look for is D-28 – Doors, Gates and Activating Devices, which CSLB defines as installing, modifying or repairing residential, commercial or industrial doors "including overhead or sliding door assemblies."

Step 1: Get the license number before anyone drives out

CSLB says every contractor advertisement — online ad, flyer, business card, even the name on a truck — must show the contractor's state license number. Contractors must also put it on bids and contracts. If an ad or a call-center agent can't give you one, treat that as your answer.

Step 2: Look it up on CSLB's Instant License Check

  1. Open CSLB Instant License Check.
  2. Search by license number (or business name if you only have a name).
  3. Confirm the business name matches the company you called and the name that will be on your invoice.
  4. Confirm the status is current/active and the classifications include D28 (garage door specialists) — or a general B license if a general contractor is doing a larger job.

CSLB's own warning: "Seeing a license number doesn't necessarily mean the license is valid. Check the license status."

Step 3: Check bond and workers' comp

  • Bond: CSLB requires licensed contractors to carry a $25,000 contractor license bond. CSLB notes it is often not enough to cover multiple complaints.
  • Workers' comp: if a contractor has employees, California requires workers' compensation insurance. A license marked Exempt means the contractor has declared no employees. If a crew of several workers shows up, ask about it — CSLB warns that if an uninsured worker is hurt on your property you could be financially liable.

Step 4: Match the person to the license

When someone arrives to bid, CSLB suggests asking for their pocket license plus a photo ID, and making sure the person matches the license. Salespeople must be registered as Home Improvement Salespersons.

Step 5: Get it in writing

CSLB recommends at least three written bids on the same scope. On home improvement jobs, a contractor generally can't ask for a down payment of more than 10% of the contract price or $1,000, whichever is less (a rare bonded exception exists).
